# From AI Time Savings to Real Business Value

**Start here:** Write down exactly what changed and what you measured. A task finishing sooner may be useful, but it doesn't by itself show that the organisation has released usable capacity, reduced costs or improved a service.

This page adapts *Measuring Released Capacity and Business Value* and *AI Value Evidence Checklist* from the private Personal AI Knowledge Base. Those are independently developed research and learning documents, not validated financial audit methods. For a first experiment, begin with [How to Tell Whether AI Actually Helped](how-to-tell-whether-ai-actually-helped.md).

## Keep six claims separate

| Claim | What would support it? |
| --- | --- |
| **AI was used** | Permitted usage records for the relevant task |
| **A task was faster or better** | Comparable cases, accepted quality, active time and errors |
| **The whole workflow improved** | End-to-end handling, queues, rework and correctly completed work |
| **Usable capacity was released** | Staff can reliably use the freed time, after interruptions and new checking work |
| **Capacity was redeployed** | More demand served, a backlog reduced or another useful activity completed |
| **A financial or service benefit was realised** | A measured outcome with relevant costs and an authorised owner checking the claim |

A task can become less frustrating without saving money. That is still a possible benefit, provided we describe it accurately.

## Make the comparison before doing the arithmetic

Record the workload and cases included, the period, starting performance and how the comparison was made. The assisted process must include prompting, source gathering, review, errors, rework, exceptions and any extra support. Compare like with like where possible, and note changes in staffing, demand, case difficulty or tools.

Look beyond averages. Severe quality misses can matter even if overall time improves. A pilot result is not automatically a sustainable operating result.

## Count costs once

Typical costs include tools and usage, setup, integration, data work, staff practice, human checking, assurance, support, monitoring, maintenance and failure recovery. If review time is included in the assisted task figure, **do not subtract it again**.

A simple *gross task-effort hypothesis* is:

`eligible cases × (baseline active minutes − assisted active minutes)`

This is **not** net capacity, a salary saving or an ROI percentage. Confirm the work is eligible and accepted, account for extra costs and observe whether saved time can actually be used. Do not count the same hour once as a salary saving and again as additional output.

## Record the status of each claim

Use **hypothesis**, **forecast**, **bounded pilot observation**, **sustained operational observation** or **financially validated result**. Explain what the label means in context. A financial claim needs an identified finance or budget owner and evidence of attributable income, avoided expenditure or real cost change. Report quality and service gains separately when they have not been monetised.

## Fictional illustration, not observed savings

A fictional worker drafts a note in five minutes instead of fifteen, but the AI-assisted version then requires twelve minutes of checking. That illustration would give **seventeen minutes** of active drafting and checking, before any other steps, versus the original fifteen-minute drafting figure alone. It does **not** support a ten-minute net saving. Neither figure has been measured in real work, and the original process may also have involved checking.

The useful question is whether **the whole accepted job**, measured consistently on both routes, became better.

## Research and limitations

The private source note discusses [Dillon and colleagues' workplace experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795), [Humlum and Vestergaard's Danish labour-market study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777), [Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond's customer-support research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) and [HM Treasury's Magenta Book guidance on AI impact evaluation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/guidance-on-the-impact-evaluation-of-ai-interventions-html). They measure different interventions and populations; none supplies a universal forecast for another organisation. The private review checked published research, not its raw datasets. The government guidance is designed primarily for government evaluation, and adapting its principles to other settings requires judgement.

The guide and fictional illustration have not been independently tested with readers or used to verify a real financial result.
