# From a Prompt to a Useful Workflow

**Start here:** Copy the brief below, fill it in for one real piece of work, and paste it into the AI tool you already use.

```text
I want to turn one piece of work into a small, repeatable AI-assisted workflow.

Task:
[What job am I trying to complete?]

Source material:
[Paste or attach the information the AI may use.]

AI should prepare:
[What draft, summary, comparison or list would help?]

AI must not:
[What must it not decide, send, change or assume?]

Human checks:
[What will I compare with the source, verify or approve?]

Output:
[What should the first useful draft look like?]

After the draft, give me:
1. The prepared output.
2. Confirmed information, pending information and conflicts.
3. What I still need to check or decide.
4. A short repeatable workflow I could use next time.
```

A prompt helps once. A workflow makes the whole job easier to repeat. It sets out what goes in, what the AI prepares, what a person checks and what happens next.

## 1. Start with a real job

Choose something you already do, not a feature you want to try.

Describe the job in ordinary words:

- what starts it;
- what information you use;
- what you normally produce;
- who uses the result;
- what could go wrong.

If you can't describe the job before you open the tool, it's probably too vague for a first workflow.

## 2. Decide what the AI is preparing

AI is usually most useful for preparing work. It can turn messy material into a draft, a summary, a comparison, a list of questions or some suggested next steps.

Be clear where its part stops. For example:

- AI can prepare a reply, but a person sends it.
- AI can identify possible actions, but a person confirms them.
- AI can organise evidence, but a person makes the decision.
- AI can suggest a structure, but a person checks the source.

That line is part of the workflow, not a warning tacked on at the end.

## 3. Give it the right sources

Use the information the task needs. More isn't always better.

Useful sources might include:

- the latest notes;
- the current version of a document;
- the relevant policy or instructions;
- a previous example of the finished work;
- definitions that matter for this task.

Leave out unrelated material and use as little sensitive information as you can. If two sources disagree, tell the AI to show the conflict rather than quietly pick one.

## 4. Build the checking step into the work

Don't wait until the end to decide how you'll check the answer. Say what a person must compare, verify or approve.

A useful check asks:

- Does the source back up every important claim?
- Has the AI made an assumption sound like a fact?
- Has it missed a newer update?
- Has it merged two different items?
- Has it included information nobody needed?
- Is the result suitable for the person who will use it?

If you can't check the result, choose a different first task or make it smaller.

## 5. Separate the decision from the draft

Many work tasks are two jobs:

1. preparing information;
2. deciding what to do.

Keep them apart. Ask the AI to prepare the information and show what's still uncertain. Leave the decision with the person who is responsible and knows the background.

This matters even when the draft looks sensible. A clear answer isn't proof that anyone has decided.

## 6. Make the workflow small enough to try

Start with one task, one set of sources and one check by a person. Run it alongside how you work now for a short while.

A first workflow might look like this:

1. Collect the current notes.
2. Paste them into the approved AI tool.
3. Ask for a draft in the existing format.
4. Check the draft against the notes.
5. Correct it and make the human decision.
6. Save the final version and note what the AI missed.

Don't add a connection to another system, automatic sending or a system change just to make the workflow feel more advanced.

## 7. Save what worked

After the first try, keep a short record:

```text
Task:

Useful source material:

What the AI prepared:

What I had to correct:

What still needed a person:

Would I use this workflow again? Why or why not?
```

This turns a one-off prompt into something you can improve. It also gives you a fairer way to judge whether AI helped. A polished output isn't enough on its own.

## 8. Try it on one real task

Choose a low-risk task you already do. Use the brief at the top, keep your current process running, and compare the result with the source before you use it.
