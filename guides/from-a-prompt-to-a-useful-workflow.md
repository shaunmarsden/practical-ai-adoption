# From a Prompt to a Useful Workflow

**Start here:** Copy the brief below, describe one real piece of work, and paste it into the AI tool you already use.

```text
I want to turn one piece of work into a small, repeatable AI-assisted workflow.

Task:
[What job am I trying to complete?]

Source material:
[Paste or attach the information the AI may use.]

AI should prepare:
[What draft, summary, comparison or list would help?]

AI must not:
[What must it not decide, send, change or assume?]

Human checks:
[What will I compare with the source, verify or approve?]

Output:
[What should the first useful draft look like?]

After the draft, give me:
1. The prepared output.
2. Confirmed information, pending information and conflicts.
3. What I still need to check or decide.
4. A short repeatable workflow I could use next time.
```

A prompt helps with one moment. A workflow makes the whole job easier to repeat. It sets out what goes in, what the AI prepares, what a person checks, and what happens next.

## 1. Start with a real job

Choose something you already do. Do not start with a feature you want to try.

Describe the job in ordinary language:

- what starts it;
- what information you use;
- what you normally produce;
- who uses the result;
- what could go wrong.

If you cannot describe the job before opening the AI tool, it is probably too vague for a useful first workflow.

## 2. Decide what the AI is preparing

AI is usually most useful as preparation. It can help turn messy material into a draft, summary, comparison, list of questions or proposed next steps.

Be clear about the boundary. For example:

- AI can prepare a reply, but a person sends it.
- AI can identify possible actions, but a person confirms them.
- AI can organise evidence, but a person makes the decision.
- AI can suggest a structure, but a person checks the source.

The boundary is part of the workflow, not an extra warning added at the end.

## 3. Give it the right sources

Use the information that supports the task. More information is not automatically better.

Useful sources might include:

- the latest notes;
- the current version of a document;
- the relevant policy or instructions;
- a previous example of the finished work;
- definitions that matter for this task.

Leave out unrelated material and use the minimum sensitive information needed. If two sources disagree, tell the AI to show the conflict rather than quietly choosing one.

## 4. Build the checking step into the work

Do not wait until the end to decide how the answer will be checked. Say what a person must compare, verify or approve.

A useful check asks:

- Is every important claim supported by the source?
- Has the AI made an assumption sound like a fact?
- Has it missed a newer update?
- Has it merged two different items?
- Has it exposed information that was not needed?
- Is the result suitable for the person who will use it?

If you cannot check the result, choose a different first task or make the task smaller.

## 5. Separate the decision from the draft

Many work tasks contain two different jobs:

1. preparing information;
2. deciding what to do.

Keep them separate. Ask the AI to prepare the information and show what remains uncertain. Keep the final decision with the person who has the right responsibility and context.

This matters even when the draft looks sensible. A clear-looking answer is not proof that the decision has been made.

## 6. Make the workflow small enough to try

Start with one task, one source set and one human check. Run it alongside the current process for a short period.

A first workflow might look like this:

1. Collect the current notes.
2. Paste them into the approved AI tool.
3. Ask for a draft in the existing format.
4. Check the draft against the notes.
5. Correct it and make the human decision.
6. Save the final version and note what the AI missed.

Do not add an integration, automatic send or system change just to make the workflow feel more advanced.

## 7. Save what worked

After the first attempt, keep a short record:

```text
Task:

Useful source material:

What the AI prepared:

What I had to correct:

What still needed a person:

Would I use this workflow again? Why or why not?
```

This turns a one-off prompt into something you can improve. It also gives you a more honest basis for deciding whether AI helped. A polished output is not enough on its own.

## 8. Try it on one real task

Choose a low-risk task you already do. Use the brief at the top, keep the current process running, and compare the result with the original source before using it.
