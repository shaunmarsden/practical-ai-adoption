# Build AI Capability Around the Work People Do

**Start here:** Choose one role and one ordinary task. Find out what the person can do with an approved AI tool, which mistakes they can recognise and what support they need. A course completion or a licence count cannot answer those questions.

This guide adapts the independently authored *Role-Based AI Enablement Programme* in the private Personal AI Knowledge Base. That document is a **design, not a programme that has been delivered or evaluated with real learners**. The approach below has not been independently validated.

## Check what is needed before teaching

For each role, ask:

- **Work:** Which recurring tasks actually involve reading, writing, judging, classifying or moving information? Which parts must stay with a person?
- **Starting point:** Can the person demonstrate a useful result and check it against a source, or are we relying on a self-description?
- **Permission:** Which tools, data and actions are allowed for this purpose?
- **Working conditions:** Does the person have enough time to practise, usable access, support and a manager who can respond to problems?
- **Starting measure:** What is the quality and total effort of a representative task *without* the proposed AI assistance?

A confident explanation of AI does not show safe use; equally, someone who rarely talks about AI may use it effectively. See [the gap between access and actual use](the-gap-between-ai-access-and-actual-use.md).

## Four things to practise

1. **Understand:** Recognise when an AI answer is unsupported, uncertain or outside the task. Don't mistake fluent text for evidence.
2. **Apply:** Use an approved tool on a small task, then check the result against an allowed source.
3. **Integrate:** Place the AI step inside the actual workflow, with a clear person responsible for checking, deciding and making changes.
4. **Sustain:** Repeat under ordinary conditions, record exceptions and see whether the change still helps after the initial novelty.

These are learning objectives, **not** a mandatory training course or a certified progression scale. People may need different starting points.

## Adapt the exercise to the role

| Role | Example using fictional or authorised information | What to examine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee handling written work | Draft a short update from mixed notes, then find and correct an unsupported claim | Source accuracy and correction effort |
| Operations or service worker | Prepare a summary of synthetic requests, including one urgent exception | Missed exceptions and total handling time |
| Team manager | Compare an AI-assisted workflow with process simplification and existing tools | Decision quality, alternative routes and human ownership |
| Internal trainer or enablement lead | Help colleagues practise an approved task, then retest with different examples | Task performance rather than attendance |
| Technical builder | Test a bounded data transformation on synthetic inputs, including failure and rollback | Error behaviour, access limits and recovery |

For an actual trial, begin with [a controlled team pilot](run-a-controlled-team-ai-pilot.md). Do not put restricted information into a practice prompt merely to make it realistic.

## A short practice record

```text
Role and task:
Approved tool and permitted information:
Starting task (how quality and effort were measured):
AI-assisted task and source used:
What the person checked without prompting:
Mistakes they noticed, missed or escalated:
Human actions and approvals required:
Total time including correction and review:
What help or access was missing:
Next practice task and how it will be checked:
Follow-up after ordinary work resumes:
```

Use comparable but **not identical** tasks for a later check, so success is not simply remembering the answer. Make practice accessible and allow equivalent ways of demonstrating the same ability.

## What counts as progress?

Attendance establishes **participation**. An explanation establishes some **understanding**. A checked example shows **performance on that exercise**. Repeated appropriate use shows something about **adoption**. Only measured effects on end-to-end work can support claims about **service, capacity or financial value**.

A person may become skilled while a particular AI workflow remains a poor fit. That is a valid outcome. Compare with [how to tell whether AI actually helped](how-to-tell-whether-ai-actually-helped.md).

## Fictional illustration, not test evidence

A fictional service team rehearses summarising invented enquiries. One summary fails to mention an exception; the reviewer identifies the omission and asks for the original source to be checked. The practical question is whether colleagues can spot and handle that mistake when a different example appears. This illustration has not been run through a model, scored or observed in work.
