# Map the Work Before Choosing AI

**Start here:** Take one ordinary case and one difficult case through the existing process from arrival to accepted completion. Write down what people actually do, where the work waits and what happens when something goes wrong. Do this before choosing a model or buying another tool.

This is adapted from the independently authored *Workflow Discovery Question Bank* in the private Personal AI Knowledge Base. That question bank is a working draft, not a field-validated assessment.

## Find the boundary

Describe what triggers the work, who needs its result and how someone knows it is finished. Name the person who owns the full outcome, not just the person currently using an AI tool. Ask what would improve and for whom. If no worthwhile change is clear, stop or gather a baseline rather than assume an AI project is needed.

## Follow an ordinary case and an exception

With permission, trace examples through:

1. **Arrival:** What input starts the work? Is it complete, accurate and available to the right person?
2. **Handoffs:** Which people, systems and approvals does it pass through? Where does it wait?
3. **Judgement:** Which decisions follow fixed rules? Which require context, professional expertise or a conversation?
4. **Rework:** What gets returned, duplicated, corrected or escalated?
5. **Completion:** Who accepts the result? Does a later team have to fix anything?

Distinguish a participant's account of the process from actual observations or logs. Do not copy protected case details into a public repository or general-purpose AI tool.

## Find the binding problem

A slow draft may not be the cause of a slow service. For instance, a report may take ten minutes to write but wait three days for missing information. Speeding up the draft changes little unless the handoff improves too.

Check case volume and variation, active work versus elapsed waiting time, mistakes, review burden, the quality standard and the effect on the people involved. Mark missing numbers **unknown**. Do not fill gaps with estimates presented as observations.

Ask whether a change to process ownership, a clearer template, a built-in system feature or a deterministic rule would work better than AI. Use [the options comparison](compare-ai-with-other-ways-to-improve-work.md) before selecting an intervention.

## Fictional illustration, not a test

A fictional team uses AI to draft supplier summaries. The draft is quick, but staff wait for a missing approval before they can use it. Mapping one routine request and one disputed request may show that clarifying the approval route would help more than a better prompt. This is a hypothetical explanation, not a measured result or a finding about a real organisation.

After tracing the work, use [the controlled team-pilot guide](run-a-controlled-team-ai-pilot.md) to plan a permitted comparison. When AI is only preparing a draft, use [the human-reviewed workflow guide](from-a-prompt-to-a-useful-workflow.md).
