# Compare AI with Other Ways to Improve a Workflow

**Start here:** Before recommending AI, identify the work that needs improving and compare at least one credible way to improve it **without** a new AI system. The goal is a defensible decision, not a high AI score.

This guide draws on the private Personal AI Knowledge Base's *Opportunity Options Comparison*, *Six-Gate AI Transformation Assessment* and *Executive AI Adoption Decision Brief*. These are independently authored, desk-reviewed working instruments, **not field-validated methods**. This simpler public guide does not replace professional risk, privacy or procurement review.

## Describe the job and the change wanted

Write down the task's beginning and end, who owns it, who is affected, what goes wrong today, what already works, and what would count as an improvement. Separate **observed**, **reported**, **forecast** and **unknown** information. Don't invent a baseline.

## Compare actual options

| Possible route | When to examine it |
| --- | --- |
| Leave the process as it is, or measure it first | There isn't enough evidence that changing it is worthwhile |
| Remove steps, clarify ownership or change handoffs | Delay comes from process design rather than from writing or analysis |
| Configure an existing tool or use ordinary automation | Inputs and rules are stable enough for a deterministic solution |
| Improve role-specific training, access or support | People lack an appropriate way to use existing capability |
| Use AI to prepare information for review | Messy language benefits from drafting or summarising, and a person can check the outcome |
| Integrate systems or allow bounded AI-driven actions | A genuine technical need exists, permissions are explicit and failure/recovery controls are adequate |

An integration isn't automatically an AI project. If an option lacks lawful access, a necessary owner or credible control for serious harm, **exclude or defer it**. Attractive projected savings don't cancel that boundary.

## Make the comparison fair

For each viable route ask: What exact result could change? What evidence supports that? How hard is it to implement and maintain? What new work does it create for reviewers, users and support teams? What happens when it fails? What would make us reverse the decision?

The complete cost of an AI option can include tools, data preparation, integration, staff practice, review, correction, assurance and maintenance. Time saved drafting isn't automatically cash saved or capacity released. See [how to measure the whole task](how-to-tell-whether-ai-actually-helped.md).

## Copyable decision summary

```text
Workflow and accountable decision owner:
Outcome sought and current baseline (or missing baseline):
What already works:
Possible approaches, including no AI:
Preferred route and evidence supporting it:
Strongest alternative and why it was not chosen:
Data, system and action permissions:
Impact on users and required human checks:
Total cost and benefit assumptions (mark unknowns):
Smallest permitted test, or why no test should proceed:
What evidence would overturn this recommendation:
Human decision, scope, conditions and date (leave blank until decided):
```

A recommendation to measure, defer, simplify, use an existing system or do nothing can be the correct result. If a trial follows, use [the controlled team pilot guide](run-a-controlled-team-ai-pilot.md). If an established pilot is being considered for expansion, the [Pilot Readiness Assessment V2](https://shaunmarsden.github.io/practical-ai-adoption/pilot-readiness/) addresses a **different** question and does not authorise deployment.

## Fictional illustration, not test evidence

A fictional office spends time moving correctly formatted details between two internal lists. Before introducing an AI agent, the team should check whether an existing import feature or fixed mapping solves the problem. If the data are predictable, conventional automation may be simpler to inspect and maintain. No comparative trial was performed for this illustration; it is a question to investigate, not a tested verdict.
