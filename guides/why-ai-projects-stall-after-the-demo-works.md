# Why AI Projects Stall After the Demo Works

**Start here:** Copy the brief below, describe a rollout that has quietly stalled, and paste it into the AI tool you already use.

```text
I want to understand why adoption of this AI tool or workflow stalled after a promising demo or early trial, not just accept the first explanation offered.

What happened:
[Describe the demo or trial, what it showed, and what has happened since.]

What we know now:
[Paste any usage figures, feedback, complaints, or observations, including anything that sounds like a simple explanation.]

Assess this based on:
- what the evidence actually shows about output quality, versus what a few people assumed or reported;
- whether the people who kept using it report the same problem as the people who stopped;
- what happened, or did not happen, to support adoption after the demo, such as training, a named owner, or updates to the normal process;
- whether people have a clear, consistent understanding of how using it affects how their work or performance is judged;
- whether anyone has actually been tracking adoption or outcomes, or whether this is the first time anyone has looked;
- what a demo can and cannot tell you about whether something will actually get used afterwards.

Give me your best explanation for why adoption stalled, and be explicit about which parts of that explanation the evidence actually supports, and which parts would need more checking.

Do not assume the tool's output quality is the problem just because a few people said so. Do not recommend a replacement without first ruling out the alternative explanations.
```

A demo shows that AI can do something. It doesn't show that people will keep doing it, or that the organisation has made that easy.

## Why this needs its own check

Gartner expects projects to be dropped in two ways. It says that through 2026, organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data. Separately, it said 30% of generative AI projects would be dropped after proof of concept by the end of 2025, because of poor data, weak risk controls, rising costs or unclear business value.

BCG found most companies aren't getting value at all. Its 2025 research across more than 1,250 companies found that 60% get no material value from AI, and only 5% get value at scale, even though most leaders rank AI a top priority.

None of these findings says the models stopped working. They all point at the same gap: what happens, or doesn't, after a good start.

The sources are at the bottom of this guide.

## What usually explains a stall

No named owner. Someone ran the demo and got people interested. If nobody is responsible for what happens next, interest fades, and nobody notices until someone checks.

Not part of the normal process. If using the tool is optional and left to habit, it competes with the familiar way every time, and it usually loses when people are busy.

No training after the demo. A demo shows what's possible when everything goes right. It doesn't teach anyone what to do when the output is wrong, unclear or needs fixing. That's the moment that decides whether someone keeps using it.

No clear word on how work is judged. If nobody tells people whether using the tool helps or hurts how their work is measured, the safe, familiar option wins by default.

Nobody was watching. If nobody tracked use after the demo, a stall can go unnoticed for months. The first number anyone sees is then a one-off snapshot, not a trend.

## What to watch for

Blaming the tool first. A few early complaints about quality are easy to hear and easy to act on. Before you treat a complaint as the cause, check whether the people who kept using the tool report the same problem.

Reading too much into small numbers. A few bad reports and a couple of good ones aren't enough either way. Look at what's being compared before you draw a conclusion.

Treating a demo as proof people will use it. A good demo proves the tool can do the job once, with everyone paying attention and an expert in the room. It says very little about ordinary use months later with no support.

Reaching for a replacement too early. If you switch tools before ruling out gaps in ownership, process and incentives, you usually get the same stall with a different product.
