# Find Out What's Holding Back AI Adoption

**Start here:** If people are not using an AI tool as expected, don't assume they need more training. Check whether the tool helps with their work, whether they can use it safely, and what happens after the AI produces an answer.

This guide adapts an unvalidated *AI Adoption and Change Diagnostic* from the private Personal AI Knowledge Base. It is a **diagnostic prompt for investigation**, not a scored maturity model or a claim about any particular organisation.

## Write down the decision

What work or service outcome is the team trying to improve? Who owns that outcome, who uses the tool, who can change the process, and what decision is needed now?

Don't use licence numbers or a general impression of employee enthusiasm as the answer.

## Check the work, people and controls

| Look at | Ask for evidence, not just an opinion |
| --- | --- |
| **Task and outcome** | Is this still a useful job to improve? What does satisfactory completion look like? |
| **The current process** | Where are handoffs, waiting, repeated checks and exceptions? Is AI addressing the actual constraint? |
| **Tool fit and approval** | Could a normal system feature, a simpler rule or an existing approved tool do the job better? Are permissions clear for this data and use? |
| **Capability** | Can users demonstrate checking a task-specific output, or have we only asked whether they feel confident? |
| **Support and management** | Do people have time to practise, accessible instructions, a route for help and clear expectations? |
| **Participation and trust** | What do people doing the work say about errors, extra checking and the effects on their responsibilities? Can they report problems without pressure? |
| **Human oversight** | Who catches mistakes, escalates exceptions and stops harmful or unauthorised actions? |
| **Results** | Has quality-adjusted work actually improved, counting review, rework, queueing and ongoing costs? |

Use permitted evidence only. Where a worker reports a problem but there is no supporting log, call it **reported** rather than observed. An absence of complaints is not proof of safety.

## Choose one response, not an entire transformation programme

- **Change the process first** if handoffs or unclear rules are the real cause.
- **Provide targeted practice or support** when a useful, permitted tool is available but people cannot yet use it reliably.
- **Run a bounded comparison** when tool usefulness or cost is uncertain.
- **Resolve permissions and controls first** when unauthorised information or actions could be involved.
- **Stop or decline the AI route** if it creates unacceptable harm or doesn't justify the work.

One team may have several issues. Identify the one decision that can be made from the evidence now, and what would change it.

```text
Work outcome and process owner:

```

## Fictional illustration, not test evidence

A fictional team stops using an AI-generated briefing because checking it takes longer than preparing the original. A manager initially calls this a training problem. Looking at accepted outputs, exceptions and review time would distinguish a skills issue from an unsuitable workflow. No comparison or observed result is supplied here, so neither diagnosis is established.
