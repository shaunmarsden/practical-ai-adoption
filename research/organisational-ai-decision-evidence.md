# What organisational AI research can, and cannot, tell us

**Prepared for this repository:** 3rd November 2026. This is a short adaptation of *What the Evidence Says About Organisational AI: Decision Synthesis* (26th October 2026) in the private Personal AI Knowledge Base. That synthesis examined **seven selected public-source reviews**, and later noted additional economic and engineering research. It was a document-level synthesis, **not** a systematic review, independent expert verification or analysis of original datasets.

The question throughout is what someone should check before deciding whether AI is useful for a particular job.

## Six questions worth asking

| Research-informed caution | Practical question |
| --- | --- |
| Faster completion of a narrow AI task does not automatically create organisational value | Does the *accepted end-to-end job* improve after checking, rework, queues and support? |
| Results differ by task, tool, experience and setting | Has this specific work been compared against the existing method on representative cases? |
| Organisational access, skills, ownership and incentives affect adoption, but there is no universal formula | What is actually stopping people using the tool appropriately? |
| Human–AI collaboration and review design affect results; more review steps are not always better | Which checks genuinely catch important errors, who has authority, and how much effort do they add? |
| More AI-generated output is not necessarily more *correctly completed* work | What happened to quality, downstream workload and cost? |
| Continued tool use is not the same thing as lasting benefit | Are quality, service, user impact and maintenance acceptable after the early trial? |

These are **questions suggested by a limited evidence selection**, not six validated laws. A positive or negative study in one environment should not be used as a generic percentage forecast for another.

## A small set of source anchors

These sources are cited in the private synthesis. They have **not been independently re-verified for this public adaptation**, and readers should check version, study design and measures before using a numerical claim.

- [NBER, *Firm Data on AI* (working paper w34836)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34836): multi-country executive-survey research on AI spending and expectations. Reported expenditure, attributed productivity and forecasts are not audited net returns for a particular rollout.
- [European Investment Bank, *Economics Working Paper 2026/02*](https://www.eib.org/en/publications/20250383-economics-working-paper-2026-02): firm-level productivity estimates and complementary investment. An estimated productivity association is not financial ROI.
- [ECB, euro-area AI investment analysis (Economic Bulletin 2/2026)](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202602_06~c9f485c478.et.html): reported investment intentions, not an all-in implementation cost.
- [Borg et al., *Echoes of AI* (Empirical Software Engineering, June 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-026-10889-1): a controlled investigation of downstream software-maintenance work. A non-significant result in its limited setting does not prove maintenance is free.
- [Hamza, Siemon and Awan, *Does Working with AI Agents Change How Developers Code, Test, and Review?* (ESEM 2026)](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESEM.2026.67): observational repository comparisons that cannot attribute all observed review changes to agent use.
- [Sawada et al., *To What Extent Does Agent-generated Code Require Maintenance?* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06464): observed patterns in code changes; not a causal measure of time or cost.
- [Tran et al., *Characterizing the Quality Profile of AI-Generated C++ in Production* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06640) and [Liu et al., *Debt Behind the AI Boom* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28592): technical warning signals from particular code samples. Static-analysis warnings and observed resource differences are not universal production failure rates or cost estimates.

This short list emphasises economics and technical operating burden because those are particularly easy to overclaim. The seven private reviews covered a wider selection of work-design, adoption and human–AI interaction studies. **The list above is not a substitute for inspecting those reviews and their primary references.**

**What is still unknown:** Whether these adapted public guides help an actual reader, or lead to a better organisational decision, has not been tested. A real implementation also needs its own permissions, baseline, affected-user input, comparison and accountable decision-maker. Don't use this note to claim a specific ROI, safety result or independent validation.

**Source handling:** Original independent research synthesis from the private personal repository was edited and condensed into this public note. No personal career records, raw private evaluation data, employer material or source PDFs were copied. The source's research caveats remain important.
