## What I would take into a first discussion

- **Which task?** Describe a real recurring job, its inputs, current steps, frequency and the result that matters. The [first use-case guide](../guides/finding-a-good-first-ai-use-case.md) helps compare candidates.
- **Why AI?** Check whether the work needs language interpretation or judgement. Fixed rules may be better served by ordinary software.
- **What evidence?** Record the baseline, sample of work, quality checks and what would count as a worthwhile improvement. A persuasive demo is not enough.
- **Who owns it?** Name the person who knows the work, the person who can approve the trial and the person responsible if the output is wrong.
- **What happens afterwards?** Decide how users will be supported, where the new step fits their actual work, what will be tracked and when to stop or review. See [why projects stall after the demo](../guides/why-ai-projects-stall-after-the-demo-works.md).

## Similar themes, different claims

OpenAI's enterprise PDF calls for systematic evaluations and involving people close to the task. Anthropic's guide covers people, process and technology, including governance and production readiness. OpenAI Academy makes the ownership and evidence required for a 90-day plan explicit. These are *overlapping recommendations*, not independently verified proof that the same method will work in every organisation.

Google's whitepaper landing page confirms that a capability framework is available, but I have not compared its internal stages with those of the other providers because the whitepaper itself was not accessible without registration.

## What this project does not claim

- No vendor reviewed, sponsored or endorsed this reading guide.
- These sources are not the tests behind this project's fictional examples and do not improve those tests' scores.
- The PDFs remain on their publishers' sites. Check publisher permissions before redistribution.
- These readings should not replace workplace permission, security review or measured trials.

If you have 15 minutes, start with the OpenAI use-case guide and write down one task you can safely test. If you are responsible for wider rollout, read the Anthropic PDF and the OpenAI Academy toolkit before deciding who owns the next step.
