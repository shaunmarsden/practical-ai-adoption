# Real Use: Editing an AI Draft Is Not Checking It

**Evidence status:** Real use. This is not a test of the current Practical AI Adoption guides.

## What happened

In one real external-facing work task, I used AI to help prepare a report from supplied source material. I then edited the draft myself before sending it.

The report that went out contained an incorrect factual figure. A colleague spotted it after it had already been sent.

I cannot say where the wrong figure came from. It could have been in the AI output, in the source material I supplied, or introduced by one of my own edits. The record does not settle that, and I am not going to guess.

What the record does settle is narrower and more useful. The final output contained a material factual error, my ordinary editing pass did not catch it, and the figure had not been checked against the source before the report left the building.

Editing and checking are two different jobs. I was doing the first and had not done the second. Improving how a sentence reads tells you nothing about whether the number in it is right.

## What this supports

- A material claim needs checking against the source it came from, as a separate step from editing the wording.
- An editing pass can leave a factual error untouched, because reading for sense is not the same as verifying a figure.
- Responsibility for external work stays with the person sending it, whatever helped produce the draft.
- A useful check is specific: take the important numbers and claims, find each one in the source, and confirm it rather than assume it.
- This is one of two lessons [Which AI Mistakes Actually Get Through](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows/blob/main/guides/which-ai-mistakes-get-through.md) draws from across all three of these repositories, alongside the finding that a general instruction not to invent something is weaker than a mechanism that catches it.

## What this does not support

- It does not establish where the incorrect figure entered the process.
- It does not show that the current Practical AI Adoption guides were followed, because they were not being followed for this task.
- It does not show that any guide here would have prevented this. That is a claim I cannot make from one case, and the honest position is that a guide only helps if somebody actually does the check it asks for.
- It does not measure how often this happens, or how often ordinary editing does catch this kind of error.
- It is not independent validation. It is my own account of my own mistake.

## Privacy boundary

No customer, colleague, employer, commercial detail, figure, source document or report content is included here. The incorrect figure itself is not published, because the finding does not depend on what it was. This note is a sanitised summary of what I learned, not a record of the work.
